# Algorithm, AI and assumptions

The Date Crew MVP compares each of ten assigned clients with 105 opposite-gender fictional profiles. For male clients, eligibility first requires a woman who is strictly younger, earns strictly less, is strictly shorter and has exactly the same Yes/No/Maybe preference about children. Eligible results are ranked by age (25%), income (20%), height (15%), children (20%) and lifestyle/location (20%). Female clients use career/income (25%), values and children (25%), geography and relocation (20%), age/height (15%) and lifestyle (15%). Every score exposes its dimensions, reasons and trade-offs; the optional core-fit filter does not alter the mandatory male rules.

AI adds value through personalized introduction drafting. The matchmaker explicitly chooses Draft with Gemini, which calls Gemini Flash-Lite on the server with the two profiles, matching context and up to five recent call notes. Contact details and dates of birth are excluded. The returned subject, message and highlights remain editable before a local save. A real hosted Gemini response was verified, then saved and reopened after reload; failure and replacement checks confirmed that existing edits are protected. Ranking is deterministic rule logic, and the three local drafting tones are templates—neither is presented as learned compatibility or as a Gemini response.

The assessment's binary, opposite-gender rules define this prototype's scope. Seeded ages, incomes and heights are supplied facts, and income is a limited proxy for career stability. Weights and thresholds are transparent design assumptions rather than validated predictions of relationships. Verification labels and generated portraits are fictional. Basic login and browser-local state support the permitted MVP; Send match opens the permitted mock email/modal workflow, and no real email is delivered. A production product would need server access control, shared persistence and broader validation. The applicant will prepare and send the application email separately.
